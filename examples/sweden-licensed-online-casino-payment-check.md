# Worked example: payment-account identity checks in Sweden

**Scope:** Swedish-licensed online gambling operators, as described by the Swedish Gambling Authority (Spelinspektionen). This is not a statement about every gambling website worldwide.

**Source:** [Onlinekasino (för spelare) — Spelinspektionen](https://www.spelinspektionen.se/spelare/spelform/onlinekasino/)  
**Date checked:** 2026-10-25

## What the official source says

Spelinspektionen's online-casino information says a licence holder should be able to ensure that the player's bank card, bank account, or other payment service used for a deposit is the same as the one belonging to the holder of the gambling account. The page says the operator may check this using BankID or a similar method.

Consult the official page directly for the current wording and applicable legal references. This summary is intended to help readers find the relevant point; it does not replace the regulator's full guidance or the law.

## What this means for a payment-method comparison

When describing deposits at a Swedish-licensed operator, do not only record which payment methods appear in a cashier. Also check whether the operator's published terms explain payment-account ownership and identity checks.

For a careful comparison, record:

- The operator and the exact page where the requirement is described.
- The payment method or account type involved.
- Whether the source refers to deposits, withdrawals, or both.
- The country and licensing scope.
- The date the source was checked.

Do not extend this rule to unlicensed or overseas operators without separate evidence. Do not infer that any particular payment provider is accepted just because the regulator's page mentions payment services generally.

## Limitations

This example illustrates how to ground a narrow claim in an official source. It is not a list of accepted payment providers and does not verify the current cashier, fees, limits, payout times, or terms of any individual operator.
